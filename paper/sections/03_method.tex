\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Study design}
We compress one generative medical vision-language model in several ways and
compare the results at the same \emph{measured} footprint. A teacher
(\expid{E0}) answers every training question once; its answers and top-$k$ token
probabilities are cached. A smaller student that shares the teacher's tokenizer is
trained on the ground-truth answers alone (\expid{E1}) or with offline distillation
from the cached teacher outputs (\expid{E2}). \expid{E2} is the starting point of every
compression arm, and its measured peak inference memory defines the budgets: B1, B2
and B3 are fixed fractions of it, set in the pre-registration before any
compression run. Budget-matched arms must land within a pre-registered tolerance of
the target on both sides, measured on the same hardware with the same protocol;
parameter counts are reported but never used for matching. Every training arm is
run with three seeds. All decisions that could favour one arm (finding list, finding
weights, lesion-size strata, budgets, statistical tests, the rule that picks the
best generic pruning arm) are frozen before the first compression run.

\paragraph{Choosing the student.}
The student is chosen before any compression run, on the validation split only, by
successive halving~\cite{jamieson2016successivehalving,li2018hyperband} over ten
candidate architectures that share the teacher's tokenizer
(Appendix~\ref{app:student-selection}). All ten receive the same short fine-tune and
evaluation; the best five are retrained with the full student recipe, the best
three with the exact \expid{E2} recipe and two seeds, and one is kept. The selection
score weights finding-probe macro-F1, small-finding recall and image reliance
(0.4 / 0.3 / 0.3), because a student that already misses small findings, or ignores
the image, leaves compression nothing to remove.

\paragraph{Training objective.}
Students and recovered models are trained with LoRA adapters on the attention and MLP
projections of the language decoder; the vision encoder and the projector stay
frozen. For a question $x$ with ground-truth answer $a$ and a cached teacher answer
$\hat a=(\hat a_1,\dots,\hat a_T)$, the loss is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda)\,\mathrm{CE}\bigl(p_\theta(\cdot\mid x),\,a\bigr)
  + \lambda\,\tau^{2}\,\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}
  \mathrm{KL}\bigl(\tilde p_t \,\|\, \tilde q_{\theta,t}\bigr),
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde p_t$ and $\tilde q_{\theta,t}$ are the teacher and student next-token
distributions at step $t$ of the teacher answer, restricted to the $k$ tokens the
teacher cache stores for that step and renormalised after dividing by the temperature
$\tau$. Probability mass outside the cached top-$k$ is not modelled; the
cross-entropy term keeps the student's mass on the correct answer. Answer-only
fine-tuning (\expid{E1}) is the case $\lambda=0$. \todo{$\lambda$, $\tau$, $k$ and the
LoRA settings once fixed}

\subsection{Prunable units}
Units are taken from the language model only; the vision encoder and the
vision-language projector are kept. A unit is a whole decoder layer, an attention
\emph{group} or an MLP channel group. Attention groups follow grouped-query
attention: group $g$ is key-value head $g$ together with the query heads that share
it, so removing a group never leaves a query head without its key and value. MLP
channel groups are contiguous blocks of intermediate channels of a pre-registered
size. A removal set is valid when every remaining layer keeps at least one attention
group and one channel group, no unit is counted twice, and layers that inject
visual features (where the architecture has them) are protected.

\subsection{Clinically weighted pruning criterion}
Let $C$ be the pre-registered finding list and $X_{\mathrm{cal}}$ a calibration set
of training images. For every finding $c$ and image $x$ we ask a fixed probe
$q_c$ = ``Is there a $\langle c \rangle$ in this image? Answer yes or no.'' and read
the log-probabilities of the two answers from a single forward pass (no
generation). For each candidate unit $j$,
\begin{equation}
  I_j = \sum_{c \in C} w_c \, \frac{1}{|X_{\mathrm{cal}}|} \sum_{x \in X_{\mathrm{cal}}} \Delta_j(c, x),
  \label{eq:importance}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_j(c, x) = \log p_\theta(y_{c,x} \mid x, q_c) - \log p_{\theta \setminus j}(y_{c,x} \mid x, q_c)
\end{equation}
is the drop in log-probability of the \emph{correct} answer $y_{c,x}$ when unit $j$
is ablated (layer skipped, attention group or channel group zeroed). A variant
(\expid{A5}) replaces $\Delta_j$ by the Kullback--Leibler divergence between the
yes/no answer distributions before and after ablation, which measures change in
prediction regardless of correctness. The finding weight is
\begin{equation}
  w_c = \min\!\left(\frac{1}{\pi_c}, \kappa\right) \tau(c),
\end{equation}
with $\pi_c$ the training-split prevalence, $\kappa$ a cap and $\tau(c)$ the
multiplier of the finding's pre-registered clinical-importance tier; \expid{A1}
sets $w_c = 1$ and \expid{A2} drops the tier. Units are removed in ascending order
of $I_j$ (ties broken by unit identifier) until the measured budget is met. Because
the footprint does not grow as more units are removed, the smallest sufficient
removal prefix is found by bisection, which needs about $\log_2 |U| + 2$
measurements. Ablation for scoring and physical removal are equivalent by
construction: skipping a layer, or zeroing a group's inputs to the output
projection, gives the same outputs as deleting the corresponding weights.

is repaired with the same recovery: LoRA fine-tuning with offline distillation from
the cached teacher outputs, on the same data subset, for the same number of steps
and with the same hyper-parameters, checked by a configuration fingerprint.

\subsection{Mask audit}
To test whether a compressed model still looks at the lesion, we mask the lesion in
images with a positive, mask-annotated finding and record how often the yes/no
answer flips. As a control we mask a region of exactly the same shape and area,
moved to a random position that does not overlap the lesion. The lesion-mask
effect is the flip rate with the lesion masked minus the flip rate with the control
region masked; a model that relies on the lesion has a large positive effect.
